# 🧭 Strategic Positioning (April Dunford Framework)

> 📌 **TL;DR**: 
> - **Never position as a "Mod"**: "Mods" anchor expectations to buggy, fragile, and free.
> - **The Winning Category**: **"The 1-Click VR Console for PC Games."**
> - **The Core Anchor**: Just as Steam made PC game installation 1-click, NexVR turns 2D-to-VR into a 1-click console experience.

---

## 🏛️ April Dunford's 5-Part Positioning Framework

### 1. Competitive Alternatives (What happens if we don't exist?)
* Gamers leave their headsets on the shelf (status quo).
* Or they waste 2 hours troubleshooting VorpX or UEVR and quit with motion sickness.
* Or they settle for a flat 2D screen in Virtual Desktop.

### 2. Unique Attributes (What do we have that others don't?)
* Universal multi-API driver interception (DirectX 11, DirectX 12, Vulkan).
* Multi-launcher auto-detection (Steam, Epic, EA App).
* Pre-Launch System Health Doctor (catches crashes before the game boots).
* In-headset curved floating world-space HUD.

### 3. Value Delivered (Why does that matter?)
* **Zero Technical Anxiety**: Play Sekiro in VR 15 seconds after opening the app.
* **Nausea-Free Immersion**: Curved HUD and FOV compensation prevent headaches.
* **Instant 50-Game Catalog**: Unlocks your existing Steam library in VR.

### 4. Target Market (Who cares the most?)
* PCVR enthusiasts (Quest 3, Index, Beyond) with an RTX 3070+ GPU and 50+ flat games who want AAA campaign immersion.

### 5. Market Category (The Context That Makes Value Obvious)
* **The Category**: **"The 1-Click VR Console for PC Games"**
* When framed as a console platform, a **$39 Founder Pass feels like an incredible bargain** compared to buying a single $70 game.
